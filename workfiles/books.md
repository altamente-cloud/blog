# The Most Important AI Books Are Non-Technical

How to think about AI? Start with these books.

I love to read. I love AI. While there are a lot of technical books that are great for learning the mechanics of ML algorithms (which are becoming obsolete very quickly, except probably pure math books like my beloved "Deep Learning" by Ian Goodfellow), I think the most important books about AI are non-technical. They are not about how to build an AI system, but rather about how to think about AI and its implications for our lives.

If anyone asks you if you know how AI works, do you think you can answer confidently?
While you may know the technical and math side of modern AI systems, it does not mean you should not ask yourself broader questions. If you think of AI only as a statistical math construct that handles uncertainty, you are probably missing the point. It is like thinking about language as a set of rules and grammar. You know the mechanics, but you do not understand the meaning.

If you ever asked yourself, "What is AI?" or "How will AI change my life?", "Can machines become sentient?" and so on, here are some books that can shape your thinking towards AI and its implications on our lives.

## Life 3.0 by Max Tegmark

The beautiful introduction into the possible life in the age of AGI and even further - Superintelligence.
Max Tegmark explores in a very imaginative way of what the onset of superintelligence means for humanity.
The concept is simple. If you don't have an ability to learn - e.g. you are preprogrammed then you are life 1.0 (like a bacteria). You can't change your firmware so to say. But if you are human then during your lifespan you are constantly updating your firmware, but unfortunately it goes to nothing when you die - you can change your firmware but you can't change the hardware. Hence the Life 3.0 is when you can do both - like a computer program can be running on different computers.

This book does a really good job telling how vulnerable the humanity is. Explores a good deal of different scenarios starting from when we all die up to the idea that there is going to be a benevolent AI when we all just chill and everyone gets UBI while robots work for us.

He briefly reviews the goal alignment problem which deserves a book of its own (and there is one on my list).

There is a chapter that I like that says the next:

> Consciousness is controversial. If you mention the "C-word" to an AI researcher,
> neuroscientist or psychologist, they may roll their eyes. If they're your mentor,
> they might instead take pity on you and try to talk you out of wasting your time
> on what they consider a hopeless and unscientific problem.

I love the C-word subject. Book explores the very feasibility of the consciousness (is there such thing)? And which is most interesting part in my opinion is discussion of IIT (information integration theory).

## Thinking Fast and Slow by Daniel Kahneman

Actually I read this book long ago but I think it deserves to be on my list since it is still pertinent in the context of AI.
Book tells how our human mind works in terms of psychology. We are as living human beings tend to conserve energy and thinking is the one of the most energy expensive tasks. Thus our brain developed a workaround. There is a fast thinking process (when you can say instantly what is 2+2 without thinking) and a slow thinking process when you invest your energy to think something through.  There are a whole bunch of life situations when your fast thinking mode is just not optimal and so your decision. Book describes how to recognize potential pitfalls and get things right.

While this book is not about AI it is pretty much about how LLMs "think" right now. They are just generating words as a kind of fast thinking. While more reasoning models show up it all begins to look like a slow thinking process.

I really like this book.

## Human Compatible: AI and the Problem of Control by Stuart Russell

I already mentioned that the Life 3.0 book touches the topic of goals alignment between humans and AI. This book takes you to the whole new level. As machines become smarter and smarter how would you give them more autonomy without jeopardizing other peoples' privacy and personal lives. We can't even align goals between ourselves!
E.g. you tell a robot to bring you a coffee as soon as possible. Robot does that breaking all possible laws just to fulfill the goal. It is a dumb example but it shows how many and many what ifs need to be taken into account. And book really urges us to do it sooner than later.

## Think by Simon Blackburn

This book is an aggregation of philosophical approaches to the mind and consciousness.
Whether you believe in God or don't - this book explores different aspects of mind.
Famous Cogito ergo sum. The consciousness as something more than physical that not necessarily belongs to your body, possibility of mind's existence both as a part of your physical body and as something that is separate of it. Philosophical theories from the most known philosophers reviewed and compared.

In the world of AI this book would inevitably make you think and compare the theories that best human minds put together with an artificial entities. "I think, therefore I am" - does it mean we can say that AI "is" now?

So in a sense if you ask yourself what mind and consciousness is - that is a great book.

## A Brief History of Intelligence by Max S. Bennett

Now this book is something! It covers the brain evolution and compares how brain functions with ANNs. This books mostly about how our brain came to be from early single cell organism to a 80B neuron computing machine. Explore what is our brain made of and what its functions are. See how evolution turn by turn gave our brain more and more abilities. Explore 5 great breakthroughs of brain evolution.
Did you know that CNN was inspired by human brain - Dr. Kunihiko Fukushima, a Japanese scientist who invented the neocognitron in 1979 (Yes your brain does convolution like things-e.g. neurons are responsible for identifying lines and shapes).
Did you know that nematode - simplest animal that has a brain of 302 neurons - can learn? (I later discovered a project that tries to simulate its brain https://openworm.org/ but as I understand it is still not covering neuromodulators and other important learning mechanisms)
So this book is not about what the mind is but rather where it is.
Very very interesting.

## AI 2041 by Kai-Fu Lee and Chen Qiufan

While previously mentioned books guide you through the reasoning, this book tries to imagine what will happen in the short term, i.e. in 20 years (the book was written in 2021). The book consists of 10 fictional stories where the onset of technology (including AI and ML in most of the stories) disrupts the usual way of life. While it is not "Black Mirror" and focuses more on the positive than the negative, it still provides a thought-provoking look at the future.
The book is written in a smart way: story first and then analysis at the end. Basically you see the challenge, get emotionally connected with characters so you "feel" the problem first, and after that the analysis makes sense because you have a premise.

Because of the author's background (Kai-Fu Lee is a venture capitalist and former head of Google China), he has a good understanding of what technology has the potential to be developed and invested into in the near future, which makes the book more realistic and less sci-fi.

There are Reddit discussions around how right the book was in terms of predictions.

## Why non-technical books are important?

With the advancement of AI, humans have gained a lot of power. Great power comes with great responsibility. You can already see ethical issues being raised and disputed by the biggest AI providers like OpenAI and Anthropic.

The alignment problem is one of the most important problems in AI. If we do not pay enough attention to it now, it may already be too late when we have AGI.

That is why non-technical books matter. They help you think not only about what AI can do, but what it should do, who should decide, and what responsibilities come with building and using these systems.
